# Parent-company name for the LLC (2026-11-17)

**Goal:** a neutral, grown-up name for the Utah LLC that publishes Tittl, PretendThat and future apps. It becomes the legal seller name on the App Store and Google Play. Context: [decisions/2026-11-17-company-rename.md](../decisions/2026-11-17-company-rename.md).

**Not legal advice.** These are screening checks, not a trademark clearance. Before filing the Utah amendment, run the two manual checks below (Utah registry, USPTO) on the final 2–3.

## What was checked, and the limits

| Check | How | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Utah LLC registry | Could not query: businessregistration.utah.gov and OpenCorporates both refused automated access (403). | **Check manually** at https://businessregistration.utah.gov/EntitySearch/OnlineEntitySearch (search the name with "Contains"). Utah rejects names that are not "distinguishable" from an existing entity; suffixes like LLC, Labs or Studio may not count as distinguishing. |
| USPTO trademarks | Direct tmsearch.uspto.gov queries were blocked in this session. Used Trademarkia's US search (it mirrors USPTO data) for exact names, plus web searches. | Exact-spelling search only. It does not catch sound-alikes, look-alikes or common-law (unregistered) use, and Trademarkia can lag USPTO by days or weeks. **Confirm at https://tmsearch.uspto.gov** (search the name and its parts, filter "Live", classes 9, 41, 42). |
| App stores | Web searches restricted to apps.apple.com and play.google.com, plus general searches. | Search engines index only part of the store. A developer name could exist without showing up. Search each finalist in the App Store app and Play Store too. |
| Domains | `dig` (NS record) on .com/.co/.app. `whois` to confirm the .com for the finalists. | NXDOMAIN on DNS strongly suggests a domain is unregistered but is not proof; .co/.app were checked by DNS only. Nothing was bought or reserved. |
| Existing companies | Web searches (general and Bizapedia/OpenCorporates-indexed pages). | Small or new firms may not be indexed. |

**Overall pattern:** almost every short real-word .com (Tallpine, Juniper Lane, Brightfold, Windrow, Plainsong, Oaktide…) has been registered for years. The names with a free .com are two-word compounds that no one has used yet (Tallwren, Kitestead, Mesawren, Larkacre). The alternative is to register "<Name>Labs.com" and keep the legal name "<Name> Labs LLC".

## Round 2: Crimson names

Checked 2026-11-17. Haylee's three favorites first.

**How it was checked**
- **Trademarks:** the USPTO's own search (tmsearch.uspto.gov, through its public search API), on the word mark only. It does not catch sound-alikes, design marks or state marks, and it is not a legal clearance.
- **Companies:** web search.
- **App stores:** Apple's iTunes search API, Google Play developer pages and web search.
- **Domains:** whois for .com/.co, the registry RDAP lookup for .app/.dev/.studio. "Free" means the registry has no record of the name; it is not a price quote.
- **Utah:** the state search page returned 403 to a script, so it was not checked.

**The big caveat: plain CRIMSON is crowded.** There are 240 live US marks whose word mark starts with CRIMSON, and 67 of them are in classes 9, 41 or 42. The ones that matter most for a kids' app studio:
- **Crimson Consulting Ltd (Crimson Education, New Zealand)** owns plain CRIMSON, CRIMSON EDUCATION and CRIMSON ACADEMIES in classes 9, 41 and 42, and publishes App Store apps. Education is next door to kids' learning-through-play, so this is the most likely objector to any Crimson name.
- **Red Lion Controls** owns CRIMSON in class 9 (software).
- **Daniel Paz** owns CRIMSON in classes 9 and 41.
- **Muddy Peak LLC** owns CRIMSON MOON and CRIMSON RITES in classes 9, 41 and 42.
- There are many game marks too: Microsoft (CRIMSON SKIES, CRIMSON DRAGON, CRIMSON ALLIANCE), Capcom, Koei, and Light & Wonder.
- The University of Alabama (CRIMSON TIDE) and Harvard (CRIMSON CONNECT) are not in apps, but they enforce their marks.

A Crimson + suffix name can still be registered, because the suffix sets it apart. But the mark will be "thin": hard to stop look-alikes, and easy for a big Crimson owner to challenge. Plan for a trademark attorney's knockout search before filing.

**Utah (manual check):** go to https://businessregistration.utah.gov/EntitySearch/OnlineEntitySearch and search "Crimson Foundry", then "Crimson Forge", "Crimson Labs" and plain "Crimson" (to see how many Utah entities start with it). Use the "contains" or "starts with" option if the page offers one. Utah rejects names that are not "distinguishable" from an existing entity, and "Crimson Foundry LLC" vs. "Crimson Foundry Inc." counts as the same name.

**Recommendation**
1. **Crimson Foundry**: the most ownable of Haylee's three. No mark, no company and no app-store seller uses it, and .co, .app, .dev and .studio are all free. The .com has been registered since 2010 with no website, so it may be for sale; otherwise use crimsonfoundry.studio or crimsonfoundry.app. "Foundry" (a place where things are made) also fits a design studio that makes things.
2. Of the rest, only Crimson Made is reasonably clear, and it is a weaker choice (a newly taken .com, generic words).
3. **Drop Crimson Forge and Crimson Labs.** Each is the exact name of an active game or app studio, with apps in the stores. That is the worst kind of clash for an app company.

**Before committing to Crimson Foundry:** run the Utah search above, and consider an attorney knockout search because of how many CRIMSON marks exist (Crimson Education above all).
