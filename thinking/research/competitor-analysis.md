## White space
- No product generates a per-game pretend-play kit; what exists is fixed PDFs or name-fill templates.
- No app assigns roles across several kids; every voice app is one-to-one.
- Household prop substitution only exists as blog advice.
- No product deliberately hands play off the screen.

## Where we are NOT novel
An animated voice mascot for non-readers (WimziPal); AI line-art colouring pages (a commodity); "screen-free AI" positioning (Curio, Yoto); pretend-play idea lists; picture checklists (in dramatic-play PDFs).

## Novelty angles (ranked)
1. Photo prop scan: the game is built from the real room (stretch; out of scope this weekend).
2. One coherent kit per game, carrying the kids' names, roles and story.
3. A game master for several kids: roles, secret missions, turns.
4. A mascot that goes quiet: it sets up, then steps back (anti-engagement).
5. Stuffed-animal cast.

## Commercial viability
- Static kits sell for $2.50–10 on Etsy, with a $79 bundle and a $9.99 editable pack.
- Kids' AI voice apps charge $5.99–7.99/mo; parent play apps charge $12/mo.
- Demand: parents want AI that keeps kids off the iPad; audio-only toys are reported at ~$860M; nearly half of parents have bought or considered AI toys (Common Sense Media, Jan 2026, via search summary).
- Risk: more than 80% of parents worry about AI toys and data, so lead with privacy. Paper and ink cost. B2B option: preschools.
