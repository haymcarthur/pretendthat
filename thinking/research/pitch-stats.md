## 1. Why pretend play matters (AAP)

- **Slide line:** "Play is not frivolous": it builds executive function, language and social skills. (AAP)
- **Alt line:** Pretend play builds self-regulation, reasoning and richer language. (American Academy of Pediatrics)
- **Source:** Yogman M, Garner A, Hutchinson J, Hirsh-Pasek K, Golinkoff RM; AAP Committee on Psychosocial Aspects of Child and Family Health and Council on Communications and Media. "The Power of Play: A Pediatric Role in Enhancing Development in Young Children." *Pediatrics* 142(3):e20182058, September 2018.
- **URL:** https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/142/3/e20182058/38649/The-Power-of-Play-A-Pediatric-Role-in-Enhancing
- **Verified text:** The abstract says "Play is not frivolous: it enhances brain structure and function and promotes executive function." The report body says pretend play "encourages self-regulation" and improves children's "ability to reason about hypothetical events". It also says dress up and imaginary play "encourage the use of more sophisticated language."
- **Confidence:** High. This is the primary AAP clinical report, and I read the full text (the AAP site returns 403 to automated fetches, so I checked an AAP-printed PDF copy).

## 2. Decline in free play

- **Slide line:** Kids' free playtime fell 25%, losing 12 hours a week of free time.
- **Alt line:** 30% of US kindergartners no longer have recess. (AAP, 2018)
- **Source:** The same AAP clinical report, "The Power of Play," *Pediatrics*, 2018.
- **URL:** https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/142/3/e20182058/38649/The-Power-of-Play-A-Pediatric-Role-in-Enhancing
- **Verified text:** "From 1981 to 1997, children's playtime decreased by 25%. Children 3 to 11 years of age have lost 12 hours per week of free time. Because of increased academic pressure, 30% of US kindergarten children no longer have recess." The same section says only 51% of preschoolers went outside to walk or play once a day with a parent (national survey of 8,950 children).
- **Confidence:** High that the AAP says this. Medium on freshness: the time-use data covers 1981–1997, which the AAP cites from Hofferth & Sandberg. On the slide, write "AAP, 2018", or credit the original study by its years.

## 3. Kids' daily screen time (ages 0–8)

- **Slide line:** Kids 8 and under average about 2.5 hours of screen time a day.
- **Alt line:** 40% of kids have their own tablet by age 2, and 58% by age 4.
- **Source:** Common Sense Media, "The 2025 Common Sense Census: Media Use by Kids Zero to Eight," press release dated February 26, 2025.
- **URL:** https://about.commonsensemedia.org/press-room/press-releases/digital-childhood-starts-at-age-two-landmark-study-shows-evolution-of-young-childrens-media-use (report page: https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/the-2025-common-sense-census-media-use-by-kids-zero-to-eight)
- **Verified text:** "their screen time remains steady at about 2.5 hours per day"; "By age 2, four in 10 children have their own tablet (40%)"; "By age 4, more than half (58%) of children have their own tablet." The report page also says gaming time is up 65% in four years.
- **Confidence:** High for the overall 2.5 hours and the tablet figures. Secondary sites put ages 5–8 at about 3.5 hours (3 hr 28 min) a day, but I could not confirm that on a Common Sense web page (it is in the report PDF, which I did not download), so it is not on the slide.

## 4. Parents use screens to buy time (the "now what?" moment)

- **Slide line:** 44% of parents of 3–5 year-olds hand over device games to keep kids occupied.
- **Alt line:** 2 in 3 parents say their young child does pretend play daily.
- **Source:** C.S. Mott Children's Hospital National Poll on Children's Health (University of Michigan Health), "Parent perspectives on play," Vol. 48, Issue 2, published October 20, 2025. Survey fielded August 2025; 710 parents of children ages 1–5.
- **URL:** https://mottpoll.org/reports/parent-perspectives-play
- **Verified text:** Parents encourage or allow phone or tablet games to "keep their child occupied while parents are doing something else" (44% of parents of children 3–5 vs. 24% of parents of children 1–2). In all, 78% of parents of 3–5 year-olds allow phone or tablet games in some situations. "2 in 3 parents say their young child has pretend play on a typical day" (68%).
- **Confidence:** High. This is a primary, nationally representative poll and I read the report page directly. It covers ages 1–5, which overlaps the lower half of PretendThat's 4–8 range.

## 5. Parents' time pressure and exhaustion

- **Slide line:** 57% of parents of young kids say parenting is tiring most of the time.
- **Alt line:** 41% of all parents say parenting is tiring all or most of the time.
- **Source:** Pew Research Center, "Parenting in America Today," January 24, 2023.
- **URL:** https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2023/01/24/parenting-in-america-today/
- **Verified text:** "About four-in-ten parents (41%) say being a parent is tiring and 29% say it is stressful all or most of the time." "A majority of those with children in the youngest age group (57%) say being a parent is tiring all or most of the time." The youngest group is children under 5. Mothers are more likely than fathers to say parenting is tiring (47% vs. 34%).
- **Confidence:** High. I read Pew's own page.
- **Related, not fully verified:** The US Surgeon General's advisory "Parents Under Pressure" (August 28, 2024) reports that 48% of parents say most days their stress is completely overwhelming, vs. 26% of other adults (data from APA Stress in America 2023). https://www.hhs.gov/surgeongeneral/reports-and-publications/parents/index.html. Many outlets report the figure, but HHS blocked automated fetches and I could not confirm it on the primary page. Check it yourself before using it.

## 6. Parent concern about screen time

- **Slide line:** 42% of parents say they could do better managing their kid's screen time.
- **Source:** Pew Research Center, "How Parents Manage Screen Time for Kids," October 8, 2025 (survey of 3,054 parents of children 12 and under, May 2025).
- **URL:** https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/10/08/how-parents-manage-screen-time-for-kids/
- **Verified text:** "About four-in-ten (42%) say they could be doing better at managing their kid's screen time." "Most parents say managing their child's screen time is a priority."
- **Confidence:** High. I read Pew's own page.

## Not included (could not verify, or not a primary source)

- **"Kids ask parents to play / say they're bored":** I found no primary, nationally representative statistic on this. The closest is a 2024 Boston Children's Museum survey, where 38% of parents said they don't have enough time to play with their child. It was a self-selected sample of about 701 mostly Massachusetts parents, so it is not representative and is not recommended for the slide.
- **Genius of Play / Fundamentally Children (2019):** About 75% of kids under 12 don't get enough active free play. This is an industry-sponsored survey of 840 parents about physical play, so it is weak for this slide.
