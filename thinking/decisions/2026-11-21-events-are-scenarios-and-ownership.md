# Events are scenarios to play out; one owner per object; prop stand-ins (2026-11-21)

From Haylee's first real test of the one-scene flow (space game, 4 kids).

**Decisions (Haylee):**
- **Events are scenarios, not one-time actions.** Bad: "Point your hand forward and zoom the ship", "make a silly wobbly face", "wiggle your finger to draw a path". Good: "The ship is wobbly, we have to make everything secure!", "A space cloud is in the way! Navigator, use the map to help the captain find a new path." The engineer's "use the Space Stuff to give it a pretend fix" was close. An event gives a situation plus a general direction to act out for a while (a minute or two), often with a prop or printout. It never asks for a single gesture.
- **The opener must set the scene** ("Pretend that…"). A generic "get ready" isn't enough.
- **No automatic repeat.** The event card stays on screen while that event is active, with a 🔊 hear-again button on the card. It's not a check button, just for kids who missed it. (Supersedes "she repeats each event once by herself" and "no hear-again button during play" from 2026-11-21-one-scene-music-beats.md.)
- **Setup checklists can't repeat jobs across roles** (kids fight over who does what), and pairings like "a sock as a planet" must stop without adding one rule per case.

**Claude's calls (Haylee asked for a general fix, not more rules):**
- **One owner per object, decided by the app:** for the playing count, the app assigns each prop and printout to exactly one playing role (fair spread). Setup coverage becomes "exactly once" (not "at least once"); duplicates are removed from non-owners; max 3 items per card. Likely root cause: jobs written for the hidden roles 5–6 were folded into visible cards.
- **Prop stand-ins table:** each library prop gets a short list of believable things it can become (sock → puppet, snake, tail…; ball → planet, moon, egg…), written once and reviewed by Haylee. The guide may only turn a prop into one of its stand-ins, and the app swaps in a better-fitting prop when needed. No extra AI call.
- **Item wording uses the pretend name** ("Hold up the planet"), not "your sock pretend planet".
- **Event card lifetime:** from its chime until the next cue (the calm-again line for a tense event, otherwise the next event); hear-again replays the same take (no new call).
- Add the three new music families (superhero-city, pirate-adventure, rescue-team) to the theme list.

**More from the same test (Haylee):**
- **End the scene early:** a way to be done that leaves the music part and goes to the finale and then clean-up.
- **Checklist buttons are never blocked:** ▶ Let's play! and ▶ The End always work. Pressing one early checks every remaining item, then continues.
- **Shown text must be speakable:** the print-mess item mentioned scissors, and the voice refused to read it ("not safe for me to say it like that"). Claude's call: app-written items never mention sharp or unsafe tools, and one shared "speakable" check runs on every line shown or read aloud, so the card and the voice always match. Read-aloud uses the exact words.
- **Music on/off (Haylee):** kids can turn the background music on and off during the scene (the music pill is the toggle). Nutmeg's voice and the chime still play.
- **Pause stops the music:** Haylee wasn't sure it failed (maybe not a bug). Verify only; fix only a real gap.
- **Nutmeg's face drifts during zoom (Haylee, bug):** when the stage zooms in or out, her layers don't move together and the face slides off the head. Claude's call: one transform on one container for the whole character; layers only animate their expressions.
- **More music (Haylee, 2026-11-21):** a second calm loop (calm2) for every family, alternated A/B by the player so the 10-minute scene repeats less; four more families (snowy-north, robot-lab, circus-carnival, race-day); the superhero and magic win endings redone. About 35,000 credits.
- **Prop stand-ins breadth (Haylee):** each prop gets a big range of believable uses (about 10–15) across all the theme families, so the table covers many kinds of games.
- **Safety (Haylee):** lines must never point kids at real hazards ("Put the pot on the stove spot" could mean the real stove). Everything in the play world is pretend and made from the props and printouts ("the pretend stove (the box)"). The safety check rewrites hazard words without a pretend tie-in and replaces lines that send kids to real stoves, ovens, outlets, water, stairs, climbing, outside, medicine, cleaning products or knives. The game plan's areas follow the same rule.
- **Prop stand-ins reviewed (Haylee, 2026-11-21):** skimmed design/prop-stand-ins.md, no red flags. Approved as is; fix single lines there if an odd pairing shows up in play.
