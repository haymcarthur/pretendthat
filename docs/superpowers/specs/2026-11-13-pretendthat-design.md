## 1. Intent and why

Kids aged 4–8 have big make-believe ideas, such as airplane, restaurant, vet or hotel. What stalls them is the setup:
- "What can I use as a steering wheel?"
- tickets, money and keycards they try to make themselves out of supplies the parent doesn't want used
- "We're set up… now what?"

Each of these pulls a parent in, and the parent often says "not right now."

**Nutmeg** is a stage-manager mouse with a headset and a clipboard who runs the whole game:
1. suggests a game
2. gathers the players and hands out roles
3. turns household items into props
4. makes a **matching, personalized printable kit** for a grown-up to print in one tap
5. leads play scene by scene on the iPad, passing the tablet between kids
6. then **steps back** so the play happens off the screen

**Positioning (from the competitor analysis):** Nutmeg is not an AI friend (WimziPal) and not a coloring-page app (Boxley). It is a **game master for real-world pretend play**. Three things here are new: a per-game generated kit with the kids' names and roles on it, role hand-off between several kids, and an AI that deliberately gets kids off the screen.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals**
- One polished, complete flow from "I'm bored" to playing, with no parent needed except to press Print.
- No reading required: every kid-facing element has a picture plus tap-to-hear audio.
- Works for any pretend game the kid names, not just a fixed list. Airplane is the demo path.
- The voice-first character interaction feels alive and polished, since aesthetics is 20% of judging.

**Non-goals (this weekend)**
- Accounts, saved adventures, onboarding, Android, multi-device play, a teen mode, photo/room scan.

**Anti-goals: failure even if it "works"**
- Keeping kids on the screen. Nutmeg must hand play back to the room.
- Storing anything about kids.
- An overwhelming kit. More than ~6 printed pages, or fiddly cutting, is a failure.
- Unsafe props (sharp, breakable, out of reach, food/medicine), or scary or violent content.
- Text a 5-year-old has to read to make progress.

## 3. Haylee's decisions (fixed)

- Native Apple app in Swift, **iPad-first**.
- Ages 4–8. Pictures plus tap-to-hear; no reading needed.
- Mascot **Nutmeg**, a stage-manager mouse that changes little between games. Talking style: **mouth opens and closes with voice volume** (option A), drawn in Figma.
- Flow: bored → game ideas → plan (what game, its theme/twist, and **how many kids**; the number decides the roles; **no names, ever**) → household props → AI-generated matching printable kit, **one-tap batch print** for a grown-up → on-device scene-by-scene play guide with picture checklists and tablet hand-off ("Pass the tablet to the Captain!", then tap the badge) → Nutmeg steps back and comes when called.
- **No backend storage, no accounts.** Every adventure starts fresh.
- AI generates each game's content. Only Nutmeg's *process* and the *scope limits* are fixed.
- Photo add-on is **out**.

## 5. Screen-by-screen flow

All kid-facing screens share one layout: Nutmeg on the left third, a content stage on the right, a big mic button at bottom center, and a small "🔊 again" button to replay Nutmeg's last line.

0. **Hello.** Nutmeg idles. Big "Talk to Nutmeg" mic. A small gear (behind the parent gate) opens settings: voice test, print test, privacy note, key mode (demo: direct keys).
1. **Pick a game.** Nutmeg: "What should we play?" Shows 3 LLM-suggested games plus "🎤 my own idea." Saying "I'm bored" brings the suggestions. Output: `gameIdea`.
2. **How many kids.** *(superseded by §12 Roles, not names)* Now: Nutmeg asks only how many kids are playing; that number decides the roles (no names, no swap). Was: Tap 1–4 finger icons or "5+" (sets Director mode). For each player, Nutmeg asks "What's your name?" The kid holds the mic, STT runs locally, and the player gets a color and an emoji. Nutmeg proposes **roles** (LLM), one per player (Director mode: roles for 4, plus the Director). Kids can tap "swap" to shuffle roles. Output: `GamePlan`.
3. **Props hunt.** 3–5 prop cards (emoji, household item, what it becomes, e.g. "🍽️ plate → steering wheel"). Each card has tap-to-hear and a big check when found. Nutmeg cheers as cards are checked. "All set!" moves on (kids can also skip).
4. **Make the kit.** Nutmeg goes into the thinking state, then the excited state as page thumbnails fly in. Shows the page grid and "Grown-up: hold to print." Kids hear "While it prints, you can color your tickets!" Output: `Kit`, a PDF and a print job.
5. **Play.** The scene list is generated with the kit (`Scene[]`, 4–6 scenes). For each scene:
   - **Hand-off card:** big role badge in the player's color: "Pass the tablet to the Captain!" (role title and role color). Nutmeg waits until that badge is tapped. With 1 kid there is no hand-off.
   - **Scene card:** Nutmeg narrates a 1–2 sentence story beat, then shows a picture checklist (3–5 steps with emoji and short text; tap to hear, tap to check off).
   - **Step-back screen:** "Go play! Tap me when you're ready." The screen dims; Nutmeg naps (eyes closed, slow breathing). Big buttons: "Next scene ▶" and "Help, Nutmeg!" Help gives one idea for what to do right now, generated in one fast LLM call.
6. **The end.** Nutmeg celebrates. Options: "Play again" (same kit, scenes restart) or "New game" (wipes everything).

**Scope limits enforced in the schemas and the prompt:**
- Kit: 2–4 item types, ≤ 6 pages
- Props: 3–5
- Scenes: 4–6
- Checklist steps per scene: 3–5
- Nutmeg lines: ≤ 2 sentences, ≤ 20 words each

## 6. Data shapes (structured outputs; all objects `additionalProperties: false`)

```jsonc
// Phase 1: game suggestions
GameIdeas { nutmegSays: string, ideas: [{ id: string, title: string, emoji: string, pitch: string }] }        // 3 ideas

// Phase 2: roles (role ids R1..R4; N kids → N roles, 5+ kids share roles via count)
GamePlan  { gameTitle: string, gameEmoji: string, setting: string,
            roles: [{ id: "R1"|"R2"|"R3"|"R4", roleName: string, emoji: string,
                      symbol: string /* SF Symbol from allowlist */, job: string, count: int }],
            nutmegSays: string }

// Phase 3: props
Props     { nutmegSays: string, props: [{ id: string, householdItem: string, becomes: string, emoji: string,
            forRole: "P1"|"P2"|"P3"|"P4"|"all", safetyOK: boolean }] }   // drop any with safetyOK=false

// Phase 4: kit + scenes (one call)
Kit       { nutmegSays: string,
            items: [{ id: string, kind: "card"|"sheet",
                      size: "ticket"|"badge"|"bill"|"keycard"|"fullPage",
                      title: string, symbol: string, lines: [string],      // never names; badges/tickets get a blank "Name: ____" line
                      bigNumber: string|null, perPlayer: boolean,          // true = one per player
                      copies: integer /* 1–8 */, colorMe: boolean }],      // 2–4 items
            scenes: [{ id: string, owner: "P1"|"P2"|"P3"|"P4"|"all",
                       title: string, emoji: string, nutmegSays: string,
                       steps: [{ emoji: string, text: string }] }] }        // 4–6 scenes, 3–5 steps

// Phase 1–5: free-form turn (STT text in, one reply out)
NutmegTurn   { nutmegSays: string, choices: [{ id: string, label: string, emoji: string }], understood: boolean }
```
`symbol` must be in `PrintSymbols.allowlist` (about 60 outline SF Symbols: `airplane`, `ticket`, `fork.knife`, `pawprint`, `bed.double`, `key`, `star`, …). The decoder replaces an unknown symbol with `star`. Pagination is done by the app: cards tile 8 per page (ticket/bill/keycard) or 6 per page (badge), and sheets take a full page each.

## 7. Nutmeg asset contract (Haylee's Figma export; updated 2026-11-13)

**Structure:** one shared HEAD placed on one of several headless BODY poses. Face pieces and ears stack on the head, so they always line up. Reference art: `design/nutmeg/`.

- **Body canvas** (1024², transparent, full-frame): `nutmeg-tail` (own layer for sway, drawn behind the body), then one of `nutmeg-body-wave`, `nutmeg-body-point`, `nutmeg-body-celebrate`, `nutmeg-body-sit`. Each pose defines a **neck anchor** (unit coordinates on the body canvas), a **head rotation** and a **head scale**, stored in code in `NutmegPose` (placeholder values until the export).
- **Head canvas** (1024², transparent, full-frame, so pieces stack with no offsets), back to front: `nutmeg-ears-normal | -perked | -flop`, `nutmeg-head-base` (head shape, cream face patch, nose, whiskers, bow, headset; **no eyes or mouth**), one face, one mouth. The head's neck point on its own canvas is `Theme.nutmegHeadNeck` (default `(0.5, 0.80)`), which is also its rotation pivot.
- **Faces** (eyes + brows, no mouth): `nutmeg-face-happy`, `-talking`, `-listening`, `-thinking`, `-ooh`, `-yay`, `-oops`, `-nap`.
- **Mouths:** `nutmeg-mouth-closed`, `-small`, `-open`, `-wide`, `-ooh`. Some faces have a fixed default mouth (yay → wide, ooh → ooh, thinking → small, nap → closed, oops → small). While TTS plays, the amplitude-driven mouth overrides it.
- Optional `nutmeg-costume-<gameKey>` on the head canvas.
- **State → (pose, face, ears):** idle → wave/happy/normal; listening → point/listening/perked; thinking → wave/thinking/normal; talking → current pose/talking/normal + live mouth; excited → celebrate/yay/perked; error → wave/oops/flop; step-back nap → sit/nap/normal.
- Export as **SVG** (or PDF) into `Assets.xcassets` with **Preserve Vector Data** on. Placeholder art ships under the same names (generated by `tools/make_placeholder_art.py`), so swapping is a drag-and-drop.
- **Brand tokens** live only in `Theme.swift`: `background`, `surface`, `ink`, `primary`, `accent`, `success`, 4 `playerColors` (colorblind-safe, always paired with a role symbol so color isn't the only cue), `displayFont` (rounded), `bodyFont`, `cornerRadius`, `shadow`. Nothing else in the code may hard-code a color or font.

## 7b. Brand and scene art (Haylee's direction)

- **World:** a backstage toy theater. Every game is a "show", setup is "getting ready for the curtain", and play starts with "Places, everyone!" The app opens with the curtain rising.
- **Style:** bold, flat vector, friendly, big expressive eyes (references: Как белка, Duolingo; Easy Chinese for Kids for energy). Not 3D clay, not chaotic doodles.
- **Palette:** coral-red (curtains), deep purple (backstage), sunny yellow (spotlight), lime (success/checked). Polish bar: Dominican Republic Dice (cohesive, rounded buttons).
- **Solid backgrounds, no full-scene art:** screens use solid backgrounds (white or a solid brand color) with lots of white space. Theater props are bundled as assets and placed around the mascot as accents: `prop-curtain-corner`, `prop-spotlight`, `prop-ticket`, `prop-star`. A screen can be a big close-up of the mascot's face on a solid color.
- **Ambient motion:** an `AmbientStageView` animates the props subtly (the curtain corner sways a few degrees on a slow sine, the spotlight pulses). It respects Reduce Motion and never competes with Nutmeg while she talks.

## 8. Privacy story (say it in the demo)

- No accounts, no database, no analytics. When the app closes, the adventure is gone.
- **We never ask for kids' names.** Nutmeg asks only how many kids are playing; badges and tickets print a blank "Name: ____" line the kids write on themselves.
- **Demo build:** the iPad calls Anthropic and OpenAI directly with a developer key; there is no server of ours in between, so nothing is stored by us. Don't claim a relay is live. Don't claim more than that about Anthropic's own data retention. Say "handled under Anthropic's commercial API terms." For the shipped app, say the plan is a stateless relay (keys off the device, nothing logged or stored); see `relay/README.md`.

## 9. Safety rules (system prompt + code)

- Audience is ages 4–8. Kind and silly, never scary, violent, romantic, or brand-name. Short words.
- Props allowlist guidance: soft or sturdy household items (pillows, chairs, blankets, boxes, plastic plates and cups, stuffed animals, towels, books). Never knives, scissors (except a grown-up cutting printables), glass, electronics, cords, food, medicine, or anything up high. Code drops `safetyOK=false` props.
- Unclear speech → `understood:false` → Nutmeg asks again and shows choices.
- `stop_reason` `refusal` or a network error → Nutmeg says "Oops, my clipboard got mixed up!" and offers a **built-in airplane fallback** (one bundled JSON of GamePlan/Props/Kit), so the demo never dead-ends.

## 12. Roles, not names (Haylee, 2026-11-13; supersedes C7 name tokens, §3 step 2, Director mode)
